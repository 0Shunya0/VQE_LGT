NOTE ON RESULT: the gauge-invariant ansatz is a strictly 1D nearest-
neighbour chain. IBM's heavy-hex coupling graph is not a pure grid -- it
contains long degree-2 'arms' between its degree-3 branch nodes (verified
directly against FakeSherbrooke's coupling_map: physical qubits 121-126
and 119-126 used below form genuine contiguous linear chains, each edge
present in the coupling map). At the 6-8 qubit sizes studied here, the
router (SabreLayout/SabreSwap, opt level 3) finds a zero-SWAP embedding
onto one such arm for every seed tried. So the CNOT/ECR COUNT does NOT
increase over the linear-chain assumption at these sizes -- the heavy-hex
penalty for this specific ansatz shows up only as DEPTH inflation, from
CX-to-ECR basis translation (each logical CX becomes 1 ECR + surrounding
single-qubit gates), not from routing/SWAP overhead. This contradicts the
premise that CNOT count must go up on heavy-hex for this circuit; a wider
qubit count or a non-chain ansatz would be needed to see SWAP-driven CNOT
growth. Reported here as found, not adjusted to match the expected story.
